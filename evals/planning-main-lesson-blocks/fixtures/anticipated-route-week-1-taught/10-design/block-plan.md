# Meteorology: block plan

## Storyline design

- **Grade and grade mode:** G12 (ม.6), Who?
- **Anchoring phenomenon (chosen by the teacher):** the arrival of the southwest monsoon over Chiang Mai: the first heavy afternoon storm after weeks of haze and heat.
- **Driving question:** "Why does the first monsoon storm come so suddenly after weeks of haze, and what does it change for the life around us?"
  - **Authorship:** teacher-drafted, provisional; the class revisited it at the driving question board on day 2 and kept it.
- **Learning intentions (PISA-adapted):** S1.4 explain phenomena with models of systems; E1.3 judge which data can support a claim.
- **Thai indicators:**
  - `ว 3.2 ม.6/10` อธิบายการหมุนเวียนของอากาศตามเขตละติจูด และผลที่มีต่อภูมิอากาศ
  - `ว 3.2 ม.6/11` อธิบายปัจจัยที่ทำให้เกิดการหมุนเวียนของน้ำผิวหน้าในมหาสมุทร และรูปแบบการหมุนเวียนของน้ำผิวหน้าในมหาสมุทร
  - `ว 3.2 ม.6/12` อธิบายผลของการหมุนเวียนของอากาศและน้ำผิวหน้าในมหาสมุทรที่มีต่อลักษณะภูมิอากาศ ลมฟ้าอากาศ สิ่งมีชีวิต และสิ่งแวดล้อม
  - `ว 3.2 ม.6/14` แปลความหมายสัญลักษณ์ลมฟ้าอากาศที่สำคัญจากแผนที่อากาศ และนำข้อมูลสารสนเทศต่าง ๆ มาวางแผนการดำเนินชีวิตให้สอดคล้องกับสภาพลมฟ้าอากาศ
- **Target ideas:** 1. Uneven heating of land, sea and latitudes drives air to move in large circulations (Air on the move). 2. Wind and the Earth's turning drive surface ocean currents that carry warmth (Ocean currents). 3. Symbols on a weather map stand for measured air pressure, wind and rain, and can be read to plan ahead (Reading the sky and the weather map). 4. The monsoon is air and sea circulation working together, with effects on weather, living things and people (Putting it together).
- **Prerequisite ideas:** 1. Warm air rises and expands (G11 atmosphere and light; checked at Recall on day 2). 2. Water takes longer than land to warm up and cool down (G9 heat; checked at Recall when it is first needed).
- **Sequence:** anticipated route. The driving question board chooses the next interchangeable unit at Recall on the last day of each unit; the fixed units hold their places.

## Unit preparation

### 2. Air on the move (interchangeable)

- **New experiences:** smoke-and-candle convection box; the class's own temperature readings of the sports field and the pond at noon; satellite loop of the April haze clearing.
- **Equipment and sources:** convection box with two chimneys, incense, tea light; 6 infrared thermometers; satellite loop saved offline (GISTDA, April–May).
- **Stands on its own:** starts from the anchor storm's wind; needs no other interchangeable unit.

### 3. Ocean currents (interchangeable)

- **New experiences:** coloured warm and cold water in a long tank with a fan blowing across the surface; sea-surface temperature maps of the Andaman Sea and the Gulf of Thailand, March and June.
- **Equipment and sources:** long glass tank, food colouring, kettle, ice, desk fan; SST maps printed A3 (NOAA Coral Reef Watch).
- **Stands on its own:** starts from the anchor storm's humid air coming "from the sea"; needs no other interchangeable unit.

### 4. Reading the sky and the weather map (interchangeable)

- **New experiences:** a week of the class's own sky observations and barometer readings; the Thai Meteorological Department's daily weather maps for the storm week.
- **Equipment and sources:** aneroid barometer, cloud chart (Thai and English), TMD weather maps printed for the storm week.
- **Stands on its own:** starts from the class's own sky record kept since day 1.
