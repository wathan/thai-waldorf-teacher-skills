# Two-day rhythm (after Zech) as the main-lesson default

Main lesson blocks default to Zech's upper-school rhythm: an experience is taken in on one day and left un-analysed; the next day its recall flows straight into discussion and concept formation (about 40–50 minutes), then earlier concepts are applied and today's new experience comes last. This departs from the four-day cycle (experience → recall → concept → apply) that the Rawson reference presents as its general model, because Rawson himself notes that recall can flow straight into concept formation in higher grades (p. 97) and a 2–4-week block cannot afford a three-day lag before any concept forms.

## Consequences

- Assessment-as-learning reflection sits right after recall and concept formation, on the concept formed that day from yesterday's experience, before Practice and New Content; never directly after New Content.
- Concept formation is an individual position integrated into the class's understanding, not a predetermined concept, so the epistemic agency audit expects the explanatory-model domain open in that phase and flags plans that script the concept as the answer.
- The day-plan template's Recall row covers recall and concept formation together; the Thai core plan stage mapping is unchanged.
- The four-day cycle remains available when a teacher asks for it.
