# Opening more: where, moves and protocols

Used only when the teacher asks how to open a plan up. Suggestions sit beside the plan; the plan is changed only when the teacher asks for that (SKILL.md Step 6).

## Where to open

Start from the audit, in this order:

1. **Scripted concepts**: turn the given concept into students' individual positions compared and integrated by the class, with the term added afterwards.
2. **Closures without a reason**: name the smallest hand-over (e.g. groups choose the variable to change; the class picks which board question the next session takes up).
3. **Openings with short reach**: extend them (e.g. the class's criteria used again in later sessions; a group's question carried to the board).

Never suggest opening the anchoring phenomenon, a prerequisite idea or a closure kept for safety. Keep the plan's rhythm: in a main lesson block nothing analyses the day's new experience on the same day. Opening a decision does not mean leaving students without support: say what the teacher prepares (materials to choose from, a question stem, a time box).

## Dialogic moves

Give each move as the teacher would say it, tied to the session's content (after Michaels and colleagues' accountable talk; Alexander; Mercer).

| Move | Purpose | Example wording |
|---|---|---|
| Wait time and writing first | Everyone forms a position before anyone speaks | "Take two minutes: write or sketch what you think happened. Then we talk." |
| Revoicing | Makes a student's idea public and checks it | "So you're saying the steeper line means more current for the same voltage. Is that right?" |
| Pressing for reasoning | Asks why | "What makes you think that?" |
| Pressing for evidence | Ties the claim to data or the experience | "Which point on your graph shows that?" |
| Inviting others to add or disagree | Turns one answer into class talk | "Who sees it differently?" · "Who can add to what Group 2 said?" |
| Building on | Links ideas across students, the board and sessions | "That connects to a question on our board. Which one?" |
| Handing back the decision | Keeps authorship with the class | "Which of these explanations fits all our graphs? How would we decide?" |

Avoid evaluating moves ("Good, that's right") at the point of an opening: they close the explanatory model.

## Discussion protocols

Pick one or two that fit the session's length and the decision being opened:

- **Think–pair–share** (any domain, 5–10 minutes): individual writing, pair talk, then the class.
- **Gallery walk** (explanatory model, 15–25 minutes): groups post their models, graphs or claims; others leave written questions or agreements on sticky notes; the class then compares.
- **Consensus discussion** (explanatory model, 20–30 minutes): the class weighs competing explanations against shared evidence and agrees what it can claim now, recording open disagreements.
- **Building-understandings discussion** (explanatory model): the teacher puts two or three students' different positions side by side for the class to work on.
- **Question formulation** (investigation aim): students write questions alone, sort them (open/closed, testable), then choose which to pursue; feeds the driving question board.
- **Planning frame for a trial** (investigation method): groups decide what to change, what to keep the same and what to measure, then compare plans before running them.
- **Criteria co-construction** (explanatory model): students look at two or three anonymous sample explanations and agree what makes one stronger.
- **Fishbowl** (explanatory model, longer sessions): a small group discusses while the rest note arguments, then swap.

## Quieter channels

Always include at least one for students who rarely speak in the whole class:

- writing or sketching first, then pair talk, before anything is said to the class;
- sticky notes or cards onto the board or a shared chart, anonymous if needed;
- a silent written discussion (chalk talk) on a large sheet;
- small groups of three before the class, with each group reporting one idea that is not the speaker's own;
- a choice to show a position as a drawing or diagram rather than in words;
- the student's first language with a partner first, for students learning in an additional language.

## Writing the suggestions

Per session named: the decision to open and its domain, its likely reach, what the teacher prepares, two or three moves with wording, one protocol and one quieter channel. Keep it to a short list per session; the teacher chooses.
