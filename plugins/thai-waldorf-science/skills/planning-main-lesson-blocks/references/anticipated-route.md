# Anticipated route

An anticipated route plans a storyline-based main lesson block as a set of units in an expected order, most of them interchangeable, arranged during the main lesson block as the driving question board directs. Only a few anchor points are fixed. Use it only when the teacher asks for it, by name or by asking for the class's questions to decide the order of units. The fixed sequence stays the default.

Everything in the storyline-design and two-day-rhythm references still holds. This file replaces only their fixed-sequence parts: units in a fixed order, the board framing ideas but not order, and New Content chosen in the fixed unit order.

## Fixed and interchangeable units

Mark every unit **fixed** or **interchangeable**. Three kinds of unit are fixed, and say which kind:

- **fixed (anchor):** the first unit, which opens from the anchoring phenomenon and builds the driving question board.
- **fixed (calendar-bound):** anything tied to a date: a booked visit or guest, a seasonal or one-off event (the first rain, a full moon, a festival), a school day that cannot move. Pin it to its day; whichever interchangeable unit is running then hosts it, so it must make sense inside any of them.
- **fixed (putting pieces together):** the last unit, which returns to the anchoring phenomenon and the driving question with what the class has figured out and closes the board.

Every other unit is **interchangeable**: it can run at any of the open positions. Give the units an **expected order**, the order you anticipate the board choosing, and its length in days (about, not exact). The expected order is a forecast for the teacher, not a sequence the class must follow.

If one unit truly needs another before it, it is not interchangeable with it: record the constraint ("after Air on the move") in the unit's row, or ask the teacher whether to merge or fix the two. Keep the teacher's own units, lengths and fixed dates when they have given them.

## Preparing every interchangeable unit in advance

The route can only follow the class if every interchangeable unit is ready on the day the board chooses it. Prepare each one completely, now, so that it works in any open position:

- its title, its question in students' terms (the kind of question likely to appear on the board), its target ideas, its Thai indicators;
- its new experiences in order (demonstrations, observations, fieldwork, data), each with what it shows and how long it takes;
- **equipment and sources** in full: materials and quantities, data sets, maps, films, texts, with where they come from; anything that needs booking or ordering ahead, with its lead time, so it is ready before the earliest day the unit could start;
- **how it opens from anywhere:** its first new experience starts from the anchoring phenomenon, the class's own observations or the board, never from another interchangeable unit's results. Name the prerequisite ideas it relies on (from the anchor unit or earlier blocks) and how Recall checks them;
- how it can host the calendar-bound segment, if its expected days could cover that date.

Never leave an interchangeable unit's preparation "to be completed when chosen".

## The driving question board chooses the next unit

- **Built at Recall on day 2**, as in the fixed sequence.
- **Choosing:** at Recall on the last day of each unit (or at Recall on the next unit's first day when the teacher prefers), the class revisits the board: new questions are added, answered ones marked, and the class says which cluster of questions it wants to pursue next. The teacher matches that cluster to a prepared interchangeable unit. Plan this moment into the unit's last day.
- **New Content follows the choice.** The first new experience of the chosen unit comes last that same day or the next, left un-analysed as always; because the unit is fully prepared, it is ready whichever unit is chosen.
- **When the board's choice matches no prepared unit**, the teacher decides: the nearest prepared unit framed around the class's question, or a new unit planned on the spot with its preparation flagged. Never decide for the teacher, and never invent the class's questions.
- **Closed** in the putting-pieces-together unit.
- If the course layout maps a `driving question board` location, say in the block plan that the record of the board's choices lives there; do not write that record (it comes from the class).

## Writing the first time

When the teacher first asks for an anticipated route, write:

1. **The block plan:** the storyline design section with the sequence stated as an anticipated route, the units table with status and expected order, a *Unit preparation* section with every interchangeable unit prepared in full, an empty *Route so far* record, then the form. Rows of the form for weeks that hold interchangeable units say what is expected and that they are written once the board has chosen.
2. **Week plans only for weeks wholly fixed in advance:** normally week 1 (the anchor unit). A week that holds an interchangeable unit is written later, by rolling generation. If the anchor unit ends mid-week, write its days and mark the rest of that week as waiting for the board's choice.

Day plans and Thai core plans are not written now unless the teacher asks, and then only for the anchor unit's fixed days: for an anticipated route they are written only for fixed weeks and units the board has chosen (below). A whole-block document set for an anticipated route therefore covers the fixed weeks now and grows with each board choice.

## Rolling generation once the board has chosen

When the teacher reports the board's choice of the next unit ("the class chose ocean currents"), read the existing block plan and week plans first, then:

1. **Update the block plan in place.** Add the unit to *Route so far* (days, unit, "chosen by the board on …"). Keep every status, the remaining units with their preparation, and the fixed dates; adjust the expected order of the units still open only if the choice changes it. Never write a second block plan.
2. **Write the week plan(s) for the chosen unit's days**, from that unit's prepared new experiences, in the two-day rhythm. Its first day opens with Recall of the previous unit's last experience, which bridges into the board's choice. If the unit ends mid-week, mark the remaining days of that week as waiting for the board's next choice; when that choice comes, fill those days in the same week plan, in place. Never fill days with an interchangeable unit the board has not chosen.
3. **Keep what is behind.** Week plans for weeks already taught stay unchanged unless the teacher asks; a teacher's review notes in them are never rewritten.
4. **Fixed units arrive by the calendar.** The calendar-bound segment goes on its day inside whichever unit is running. When the open positions are used up, the putting-pieces-together unit is written next without a board choice.
5. **Day plans and Thai core plans for the chosen unit** are written only now, never for a unit the board has not chosen: day plans through the skill's day-plans route (SKILL.md Step 0) for the chosen unit's days when the teacher asks for them, Thai core plans by `writing-thai-core-plans`. Offer them in the reply; do not write them unasked.

If the teacher asks for plans of an interchangeable unit the board has not chosen, say that it is written once the board chooses it, and point to its preparation in the block plan, which is already complete.

## Block plan sections for an anticipated route

In the storyline design section, replace the sequence line:

```markdown
- **Sequence:** anticipated route. The driving question board chooses the next interchangeable unit at Recall on the last day of each unit; fixed units hold their places.

| Unit | Status | Expected position | Students' likely question | Target ideas | Thai indicators | Days |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. … | fixed (anchor) | days 1–5 | … | … | … | 5 |
| 2. … | interchangeable | expected 2nd | … | … | … | about 4 |
| Visit to … | fixed (calendar-bound) | Wednesday of week 3 | … | … | … | 1 |
| 5. … | fixed (putting pieces together) | last 3 days | … | … | … | 3 |
```

Then, still above the form:

```markdown
## Unit preparation

### 2. … (interchangeable)

- **Question in students' terms:** …
- **New experiences:** 1. … 2. …
- **Equipment and sources:** … (book or order by …)
- **Opens from:** the anchoring phenomenon / the class's own observations; relies on prerequisite ideas …

## Route so far

| Days | Unit | Chosen how |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 (week 1) | 1. … | fixed (anchor) |
```

If the course layout has a `unit plans` row, the preparation of each unit goes there (one per unit) and the block plan links to it instead.

## Checklist for an anticipated route

Use these in place of the main checklist's fixed-order item; every other item still holds.

- [ ] The teacher asked for an anticipated route; otherwise the main lesson block is a fixed sequence.
- [ ] Every unit is listed in an expected order and marked fixed (anchor, calendar-bound or putting pieces together) or interchangeable.
- [ ] Calendar-bound segments are pinned to their dates.
- [ ] Every interchangeable unit has complete preparation (new experiences, equipment, sources, lead times) that works in any open position.
- [ ] The plan says when and how the driving question board chooses the next unit.
- [ ] Week plans, and any day plans or Thai core plans, exist only for fixed weeks and for units the board has chosen; nothing is filled in ahead of the board.
- [ ] On rolling generation, the block plan's route record is updated in place and plans for weeks already taught are unchanged.
