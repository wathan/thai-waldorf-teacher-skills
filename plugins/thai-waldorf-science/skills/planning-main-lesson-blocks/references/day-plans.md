# Day plans and the four-day cycle

How to write Waldorf day plans for a main lesson block on request, one day, one week or the whole block at a time, in the two-day rhythm by default or in Rawson's four-day cycle when the teacher asks for it. Read with `two-day-rhythm.md`, which holds the rules for each part of the day; this file says how they land on the day-plan form.

## Before writing

1. **Scope.** Write exactly what was asked: one day, one week, or the whole block. If the request leaves it open, ask once with a recommendation ("the next week, recommended"). Never add days nobody asked for.
2. **Block plan and week plans first.** Day plans unfold the week plan's day rows. Read the block plan and the week plan for every week in scope. If the block plan or a needed week plan is missing, say so in one sentence and write those first (Steps 1–6 of the skill), then the day plans; if the anchoring phenomenon is still open, write nothing (Step 2 of the skill).
3. **Follow the week plan.** Each day plan takes that day's objectives, new experience, practice and UDL options from its week-plan row, in the same order. Fill in detail (timing, questions, setup); do not swap experiences or reorder the week. If a change is needed, say so and change the week-plan row too, so the two never disagree.
4. **Number days across the block.** Block day 1 is the first session of week 1; Monday of week 2 in a five-day week is block day 6. Name each day plan by week and day, e.g. "Week 2, day 6 (Mon)".

## Where and which form

- **Where.** Use the layout's `session plans` row. A folder location gets one file per day, `week-1-day-1.md`, `week-1-day-2.md`, …; a file location holds one section per day in order. If a day plan already exists there, update it in place and keep the teacher's decisions. With no `session plans` row, run course-layout Steps 2–4 for this concept only (usual area: design) and write after the teacher confirms (or has said up front they accept your proposal).
- **Which form.** Course-layout's "Plan templates: the course's own come first" for the day plan: the course's own day form if the templates location has one (its rows and labels win), else the plugin's `${CLAUDE_PLUGIN_ROOT}/references/waldorf-plan-template-day.th.md`. Say in one line which one you used.
- **Language.** As for the block and week plans: English by default, Thai official terms verbatim, student-facing wording (recall questions, prompts) in the class profile's language of student materials, the form's bilingual labels kept.

## Filling the day form

The header copies the week plan: date (or "Week n, day d" when the calendar is unknown), block, weekly objectives, key skills. Times add up to the class profile's session length (100 minutes by default); give each part a time.

The form's rows may be reordered or repeated with their labels kept. In the two-day rhythm the rows run:

| Row label (kept) | Holds |
|---|---|
| Rhythmic Time: กิจกรรมวงกลม | Attunement, linked to the block where possible |
| Maths/Literacy | A short quantitative or scientific-writing strand tied to the block, or `—` |
| Recall: การทบทวนสิ่งที่เรียนไป | Recall of *yesterday's* experience flowing into discussion and concept formation (about 40–50 minutes), then the short assessment-as-learning reflection on the concept formed. Start the Activity cell with `Recall + concept formation (yesterday: …)` |
| Practice: ช่วงทำงาน | Application of concepts formed on earlier days: one shared task every student can start |
| New Content: เรื่องใหม่ | Today's new experience, taken in and left un-analysed; the cell ends with `Close: …`, the preview of tomorrow |

Cell rules:

- **Activity** in Recall: two or three open recall questions as they would be asked ("What did you notice first when…?"), how students take an individual position (sketch model, claim in their own words, written first, then pairs, then class), and how the class integrates positions. Name the target idea the class works toward; never write the concept students must arrive at.
- **Activity** in New Content: what students do with the experience (watch, handle, record, draw, wonder), and nothing that interprets it: no "why" questions, no worksheet questions about it, no explanation.
- **Objective:** each serves one weekly objective, phrased as figuring out.
- **Differentiation:** two or three light universal UDL options for that part, from `two-day-rhythm.md`; no ability tiers.
- **Assessment:** what the teacher looks for (main lesson book entries, positions taken, questions added to the driving question board); reflection prompts never scored; never a quiz at Recall.
- **Preparation:** equipment, setup and safety for that part.
- **Homework**, **Review** and **To do:** Homework only if the week plan has it; Review left for the teacher (write it only from reflections they supply); To do lists what tomorrow's Recall needs (photos of today's board, the demo set up again for reference).
- **Notes:** a departure the teacher chose (see the warning below), in one line.

## Day 1, steady-state days and the last day

- **Block day 1:** the Recall row holds the link to the previous block in this subject instead of a recall (named from the course if possible, else "previous block in this subject, teacher to name"): an open look back and how it leads here, then a short preview of the journey. Practice may be main lesson book set-up. New Content is the anchoring phenomenon, left un-analysed. Close: tomorrow starts from what you experienced.
- **Day 2:** Recall of the anchoring phenomenon flows into students writing their own questions and the class building the driving question board, then concept formation as far as it goes.
- **Steady-state days:** as in the table. Monday recalls Friday; the first day of a unit recalls the previous unit's last experience and bridges to the new unit on the board.
- **Last day of the block:** no New Content (mark the row `—`); Recall and concept formation on yesterday's experience, then putting the pieces together at the anchoring phenomenon and the driving question, the block reflection and students' own summary, in the Practice row.

## The four-day cycle, on request

Only when the teacher asks for Rawson's four-day cycle (for a day, a week or the block). Each experience E*n* runs: day of experience → next day **recall** → the day after **concept formation** → the day after that **apply**. Cycles overlap, so a steady-state day holds Recall E(n−1), concept formation on E(n−2), application of C(n−3), and the new experience E(n).

- **Labels are allowed** here and help: name each part with its experience, e.g. `Recall E4 (yesterday: turntable)`, `Concept E3 (sea-breeze box)`, `Apply C2`, `New experience E5`.
- **Row mapping:** the Recall row holds recall of E(n−1) (open questions only, no concept yet) and then concept formation on E(n−2), each named; Practice holds Apply C(n−3); New Content holds E(n) with the close. Rawson's figure lists the new experience before application; keep it last as above unless the teacher wants Rawson's order, and even then never analyse it.
- **Ramp-up:** day 1: link to previous block, E1. Day 2: Recall E1, E2. Day 3: Recall E2, Concept E1, E3. Day 4: Recall E3, Concept E2, Apply C1, E4. From day 5 the full pattern.
- **Wind-down:** an experience needs two more sessions for its concept, so the last two days of a block bring no new experience; the second-to-last day recalls and forms concepts on what remains, and the last day forms the final concept and puts the pieces together.
- **Keep documents in step.** If the week plans are in the two-day rhythm, update the matching week-plan rows to the four-day cycle (or tell the teacher they still show the two-day rhythm and offer to align them). Say plainly in the reply that the four-day cycle is used at their request and that concepts form a day later than in the default rhythm.
- Everything else holds: open-question recall, concept formation as an individual position integrated by the class, the new experience un-analysed on its own day, light UDL options, no tiers.

## Warning: analysing the day's new experience

Check every day plan you write, and any day plan or request the teacher brings, for a session that discusses, analyses, explains or tests that day's new experience: "why" questions about it, a worksheet explaining it, a reflection or exit card on it, a discussion straight after it, or a close that explains it.

When you find one, warn the teacher plainly in the reply, naming the day and the part: "On Monday the class would explain the cloud chamber the same day they saw it. In the two-day rhythm a new experience is left to settle overnight; tomorrow's Recall picks it up and concept formation follows from there." Propose the fix: move the discussion or worksheet to the next day's Recall and concept formation, and let today end with observation, drawing and wondering.

- In plans you write on your own, never do it.
- If the teacher asked for it, write the plan the rhythm's way with the discussion moved to the next day, and say so; if they have already insisted after the warning, write it their way and record the departure in the day plan's Notes ("Same-day discussion of the new experience at the teacher's request; departs from the two-day rhythm").
- A teacher's existing day plan with the problem: warn and offer to fix it; never change it unasked.

## Checklist for day plans

- [ ] Exactly the days asked for; each follows its week-plan row; the week plan and the day plans agree.
- [ ] Files sit at the `session plans` location; the course's day form if it has one, otherwise the plugin's, labels kept.
- [ ] Day 1 links to the previous block; every later day's Recall row covers recall of yesterday's experience and concept formation (or, in the four-day cycle, recall and the labelled concept of the day before).
- [ ] Recall is open questions; concept formation is an individual position integrated by the class.
- [ ] New experience last, left un-analysed; close previews tomorrow; no reflection right after New Content; last day has no new experience.
- [ ] Four-day labels only when the teacher asked for the four-day cycle.
- [ ] Any same-day analysis found in a request or plan was warned about in the reply.
- [ ] UDL options in every part that needs them; no tiers; no student names.
