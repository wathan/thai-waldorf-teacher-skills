# Walkthrough of the design and teaching process

Give this walkthrough in order, in plain teacher language, shortened to what the teacher needs. Keep every step's skill name so the teacher can ask for it. Skip nothing silently: if a step is optional, say so.

## The shape of the work

Each course folder holds one teaching sequence for one class: a main lesson block (2–4 weeks of daily sessions on one subject such as meteorology) or a subject lesson (recurring sessions across a term, such as Science Practice). Every sequence answers to three frameworks at once (Thai indicators, the Waldorf upper-school curriculum and its grade modes, PISA-adapted competencies) and is documented twice for main lesson blocks: Waldorf plans for the school, a Thai core plan for supervisors.

## Steps

1. **Describe the class** — `setting-up-class-profiles`. A short interview, round by round, with a recommended answer for each question: grade and grade mode (G9 What, G10 How, G11 Why, G12 Who), session lengths and weekly pattern, learner variability as anonymised counts (never names), languages, academic year in CE and ปีการศึกษา. It follows the teacher's existing course folder layout, or sets one up from the default course template after the teacher confirms, and copies the four plan templates in so the course can adapt them. Optionally records a year plan. Without a class profile, later skills ask at most three questions instead.

2. **Map the curriculum** — `mapping-curriculum`. Builds the curriculum crosswalk once (Thai indicators, Waldorf placement, PISA-adapted competencies, each in its own wording, gaps shown), then writes which Thai indicators this teaching sequence claims. Indicators from another ม. grade are cited exactly as written and justified by grade mode. On request, checks a whole year plan for covered, missing and duplicated indicators.

3. **Plan the teaching sequence** — one of:
   - `planning-main-lesson-blocks`: a storyline-based block. The teacher chooses the anchoring phenomenon from offered candidates; the driving question's authorship is stated; ideas are sorted into target ideas and prerequisite ideas. Units run as a fixed sequence by default, or as an anticipated route steered by the driving question board. Days follow the two-day rhythm: an experience one day left un-analysed, recall flowing into concept formation the next. The block plan and week plans come first; day plans one day or week at a time on request.
   - `planning-subject-lessons`: 5E inquiry by default, each unit one topic of one or more 5E cycles, phases placed across long and short sessions as the design needs, with explicit instruction, graded practice and retrieval practice where they help.

4. **Write the Thai core plan** — `writing-thai-core-plans`. The แผนการจัดการเรียนรู้ for supervisors: one per main-lesson day, or one per 5E cycle. K-P-A objectives from the indicators, ขั้นนำ / ขั้นสอน / ขั้นสรุป filled from the design, official Thai skill and attitude names, scoring tables left blank, kept consistent with the Waldorf plans.

5. **Design for learner variability** — `differentiating-for-learner-variability`. UDL options for everyone first, then targeted supports for anonymised needs; one shared task every student can start, with room to extend; the core learning intention never lowered.

6. **Plan formative assessment** — `planning-formative-assessment`. Assessment for learning (hinge questions, misconception-based checks, main lesson book work as evidence) and assessment as learning (metacognitive prompts, self- and peer assessment, single-point rubrics, never scored). Recall is open questions, never a quiz; reflection never sits directly after the day's new experience.

7. **Audit epistemic agency** — `auditing-epistemic-agency`. Runs after any plan unless switched off, or on its own: reports session by session which epistemic decisions (investigation aim, investigation method, explanatory model) the plan opens or closes, with reasons. It reads the plan only and never rewrites it; ask it for discussion protocols and dialogic moves to open more.

8. **Prepare to teach** — `preparing-to-teach`. The day before a session: anticipated student responses and misconceptions paired with dialogic follow-ups, timings, equipment and setup, risk notes.

Steps 5–7 can be run in any order once a plan exists; step 4 can follow step 3 at once or wait until the plan settles. For an anticipated route, steps 3–4 roll forward as the driving question board chooses each next unit.

## Close

Ask where the teacher is now (no class profile yet, planning, documenting, teaching tomorrow), and offer `guiding-the-workflow` again whenever they lose the thread.
