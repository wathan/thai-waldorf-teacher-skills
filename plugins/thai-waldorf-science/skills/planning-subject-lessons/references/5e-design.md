# 5E design for subject lessons

5E inquiry is IPST's recommended inquiry cycle and the default design for subject lessons. A unit is one topic made of one or more 5E cycles; how the phases fall across sessions and onto the Thai core plan's stages follows the design, never a fixed mapping.

## The five phases

Use the English names in plans and IPST's Thai names wherever Thai is written.

| Phase | IPST Thai name | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Engage | ขั้นสร้างความสนใจ | A phenomenon, problem or challenge that raises the cycle's question; students say what they notice, already think and want to find out. Links to earlier cycles. Never tells the answer |
| Explore | ขั้นสำรวจและค้นหา | Students investigate: observe, measure, test, collect and organise data, with time to try and to be wrong |
| Explain | ขั้นอธิบายและลงข้อสรุป | Students explain from their own evidence first, compare explanations and reach a conclusion as a class; then the teacher adds terms, formulae and the accepted explanation, connected to what students found |
| Elaborate | ขั้นขยายความรู้ | Students apply and extend the idea to new cases, problems or investigations |
| Evaluate | ขั้นประเมิน | Evidence of what students now understand and can do, for the teacher and for the students; it can steer the next step or close the cycle |

## Units and cycles

- **A unit is one topic.** Its size follows the indicators it claims and the class's sessions: often two to four weeks.
- **One or more 5E cycles per unit.** One cycle when the topic is one question; more when it grows step by step (e.g. measuring a circuit, then Ohm's law). Each cycle has its own question in students' terms, its target ideas, its indicators (a subset of the unit's), and its sessions.
- **Cycle length follows the work,** usually two to four sessions; a cycle may begin or end mid-week. Each cycle gets one Thai core plan, so keep a cycle a coherent piece of learning.
- **Target ideas and prerequisite ideas** are listed separately in the unit plan: target ideas are figured out in the cycles, never taught beforehand; prerequisite ideas are assumed, with where they come from and how the first Engage or a retrieval task checks them (an open question, not a quiz).
- **Keep the teacher's units, cycles and order** when they have given them.

## Placing phases on the weekly pattern

Start from the design, then lay it on the calendar. List the unit's sessions in calendar order from the class profile (e.g. Tuesday 100 min, Friday 50 min, Tuesday 100 min …; or Monday 50, Wednesday 50, Thursday 100; or one 150-minute session a week). Then, cycle by cycle:

1. **Estimate each phase's time from its work,** not from a template: a practical Explore may need 60–90 minutes; an Engage 10–20; an Explain discussion 30–50; graded practice 20–40; a written Evaluate 15–35.
2. **Place phases in order across the sessions** where their work fits:
   - **Long sessions** suit work that must not be cut: practical Explore, fieldwork, an Elaborate investigation, a practical Evaluate task.
   - **Short sessions** suit work that holds in a short span: Engage, finishing data analysis, an Explain discussion on data already collected, graded practice, retrieval practice (consolidation), a written Evaluate.
   - **A phase may span sessions** (Explore started in a long session and finished in the next short one); **a session may hold several phases** (Explain then Elaborate).
   - **Gaps between sessions** cut across the work: when a phase continues after a gap, open the next session with a short retrieval of where the class got to (from memory, then checked). After a long gap (e.g. one session a week), every session opens this way.
   - **The weekly pattern is not the design.** Do not force "Engage and Explore in the long session, Explain in the short one" every week; each cycle's split follows its own work. Two cycles in a unit usually fall differently.
3. **Give each placement a short reason** in the unit plan's session overview ("Explore in the long session: the circuits need 70 minutes of uninterrupted measuring").
4. **Check the sums:** each session's minutes add up to its length from the class profile.

Equal-length sessions, double periods, three or more sessions a week, a single weekly session or a term with a holiday gap all work the same way: list the real sessions, then place the phases.

## Explicit instruction inside Explain and Elaborate

When the unit needs a skill, procedure or piece of knowledge that inquiry alone will not secure (reading a meter, using a microscope, a calculation, a graph convention, a safety routine, a formula), teach it explicitly inside **Explain** (once students have met the need in Explore) or **Elaborate** (where they use it):

1. **State the step's purpose** in one line, linked to the cycle's question.
2. **I do:** the teacher models the procedure or a worked example, thinking aloud, step by step.
3. **We do:** the class does the next example together, with the teacher prompting; check for understanding (a hinge question, mini-whiteboards) before letting go.
4. **You do:** students practise on their own or in pairs, with the step card or worked example still available, then without it.

Keep it short (10–25 minutes) and placed after students' own explanations, so it formalises what they found rather than replacing it. Explicit instruction never moves into Engage or Explore.

## Graded practice

Practice inside Explain or Elaborate rises in steps:

- **From worked to independent:** worked example → completion (faded) example → problem with a step card → problem on their own.
- **From simple to complex:** one-step to multi-step; familiar to new contexts (a household bill after classroom resistors).
- **From blocked to mixed:** problems of one type first, then mixed with earlier cycles' types.
- **One shared task every student can start,** with entry points and room to extend; never ability tiers. Students start at the step they can and move on.

## Retrieval practice for short consolidation sessions

A short session that consolidates (rather than starting new practical work) gets retrieval practice, often 5–15 minutes, and the start of any session after a gap can too:

- **From memory first,** then checked: brain dump on the last cycle, draw-and-label from memory (the circuit, the apparatus), two or three questions mixing this cycle with earlier ones, explain-to-a-partner.
- **Spaced and mixed:** include ideas from earlier cycles and units, not only last session's.
- **Low-stakes, never scored;** feedback straight after (check against notes or the class's record).
- Retrieval practice consolidates; it never replaces Explore or the students' own explanations in Explain.

## Assessment in the cycle

- **Evaluate** can sit at the end of a cycle (a practical task, a written explanation) or partly inside it (a hinge question that steers Elaborate). Scored work uses a rubric on work the cycle already produces.
- **Assessment as learning** (exit-slip reflections, self-assessment): allowed at the end of any session, private and written first ("What I can now explain, and what is still unclear"), never scored.
- Retrieval practice and hinge questions are assessment for learning: not scored.

## Light universal UDL options

Every session carries a few options for everyone, beside the step they serve: ways in (picture or object prompts, demonstrations, word walls in both languages), ways to act and express (say, write, draw, table or diagram; sentence starters; roles in group work), ways to engage (choice of example or context, a visible step card). One shared task every student can start; targeted supports for the class's anonymised needs are `differentiating-for-learner-variability`'s work.

## Thai core plan stages per cycle

Each cycle has one Thai core plan, written later by `writing-thai-core-plans` from the cycle plan. Record in the cycle plan how its phases fall on the three stages, as this cycle runs them:

- **ขั้นนำเข้าสู่บทเรียน:** what opens the cycle, usually Engage, with any opening retrieval practice.
- **ขั้นสอน/ขั้นจัดกิจกรรม:** the cycle's main work in order, usually Explore, Explain (with any explicit instruction) and Elaborate (with graded practice); Evaluate parts that steer teaching sit here too.
- **ขั้นสรุป:** what closes the cycle: the class's or students' own summary, a closing Evaluate task, the exit slip.

This is a usual shape, not a rule: map what the cycle actually does where it does it, and say so.

## Unit plan format

```markdown
# Unit <n>: <topic> (หน่วยการเรียนรู้ที่ <n> <Thai title>)

- **Design:** 5E inquiry, <k> cycles. Weeks <a>–<b>, <m> sessions (<weekly pattern>).
- **Grade and grade mode:** G<n> (ม.<n>), <mode>
- **Learning intentions (PISA-adapted):** S<x>.<n> …
- **Thai indicators:** `ว x.x ม.n/k` <Thai wording verbatim> (sequencing difference, if any, with its grade-mode reason)
- **Target ideas:** 1. … (cycle 1) 2. … (cycle 2)
- **Prerequisite ideas:** 1. … (from …; checked by …)
- **Explain-phase closures:** what the teacher introduces after students' explanations (terms, formulae, conventions) and why.

| Cycle | Question in students' terms | Sessions | Target ideas | Indicators |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## Session overview

| Session | Day and length | Cycle | Phases | Why placed here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
```

## Cycle plan format

```markdown
# Unit <n>, cycle <k>: <title>

Sessions <a>–<b> · <total> minutes · indicators `ว x.x ม.n/k`, …

## Thai core plan stages

- **ขั้นนำ:** …
- **ขั้นสอน:** …
- **ขั้นสรุป:** …

## Session <a> · <day> week <w> · <length> minutes

| Phase | Minutes | What happens | UDL options |
|---|---|---|---|
```

Under each session table add the equipment and preparation it needs, and any safety note. Student-facing prompts and questions go in the class profile's language of student materials.

## Never

- Explain before Explore, or Engage that tells the answer.
- The concept or formula stated as what students will be told before they have explained their own evidence.
- A fixed phase split repeated for every week regardless of the work.
- Retrieval practice, hinge questions, exit slips or self-assessment scored.
- Ability tiers, or student names and identifying details.
- Waldorf block, week or day plans for a subject lesson.
