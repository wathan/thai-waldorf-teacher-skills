# Assessment as learning

Students monitoring and judging their own learning against criteria they understand (glossary). **Never scored**: no points, marks, levels or grades, and no column in any scoring table. It is not counted as uptake by the epistemic agency audit; it concerns individual learning, not the class's knowledge work.

## Metacognitive prompts

After metacognitive-prompt-library: short prompts that turn students' attention to their own thinking, matched to the moment. Write them in the language of student materials; one prompt per reflection, two at most.

| Moment | Example prompts |
|---|---|
| After recall and concept formation (main lesson default) | "What do I now think is going on, and what am I still unsure of?" · "What changed in my idea today, and what changed it?" |
| On Practice (end of day only when Practice follows New Content) | "Which step of today's work was hardest, and how did I get past it?" |
| Working process (at Recall) | Rawson's three steps: "What happened, and what should have happened?" → "What surprised me, what was different?" → "What will I do better next time?" |
| End of block (last day, at Recall) | The three steps on the whole main lesson block, with the main lesson block's criteria in view |
| Exit slip (subject lessons, session end) | "What I can now explain… / What is still unclear…" · "One question I still have" |

Grade mode can tune the prompt: G9 (What) on what was observed; G10 (How) on how the work was done; G11 (Why) on reasons and evidence; G12 (Who) on the student's own stance and responsibility.

## Self-assessment

Against criteria students understand: the block sheet's criteria where the course has one, otherwise two to four criteria the plan states in students' words. Private written channel first (see the channels in `placement-rules.md`). The teacher may read it to plan; it is never marked.

**Student-set criteria** (the class decides what counts as good evidence or a good explanation) are an opening in the explanatory-model domain: note it in the plan for the epistemic agency audit.

## Peer assessment

Only after the criteria are shared: given on the block sheet, or agreed with the class before work is exchanged. Peers give feedback against the criteria (one strength, one question or next step), privately in writing on a slip or in the margin first; spoken peer feedback only in pairs. Peers never score each other. When a teacher asks for peer scoring, plan peer feedback against shared criteria instead and say why in one line: scores turn assessment as learning into judgement and make kreng jai louder.

## Single-point rubrics

After single-point-rubric-designer: one middle column of criteria, each written as the expected quality; blank columns either side for the student's own notes. No levels, no points.

| ยังต้องพัฒนา (my notes) | Criterion: what good looks like | ทำได้เกินเกณฑ์ (my notes) |
|---|---|---|
| | Explanations use evidence from our own measurements | |

Column labels and criteria go in the language of student materials (Thai labels as above by default; English labels "Still working on" and "Beyond the criterion" for an English class).

## In the Thai core plan

`writing-thai-core-plans` names assessment as learning in the measurement section as การประเมินเพื่อการเรียนรู้ของตนเอง with "ไม่ให้คะแนน" (not scored), and gives it no column in the scoring tables. The assessment plan says so in its notes for the Thai core plan, so the two stay consistent; this skill does not write the Thai core plan.
