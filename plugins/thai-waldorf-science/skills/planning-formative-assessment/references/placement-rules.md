# Placement rules

Where each assessment item may sit in a session, and what to flag in the teacher's plan. The two-day rhythm (ADR-0003; `planning-main-lesson-blocks`) is the frame for main lesson blocks: today's new experience is taken in and left un-analysed; its recall flows into concept formation tomorrow.

## Main lesson blocks

| Part of the day | Assessment for learning | Assessment as learning |
|---|---|---|
| Recall | Open questions only ("What did you notice when…?", "What surprised you?"); the teacher listens for what students hold and what they missed. **Never a quiz**, a test or closed questions with one right answer | Reflection on working process and the end-of-block reflection, with Rawson's three steps (below) |
| Concept formation | Positions students take (claim, sketch model) as evidence; a hinge question or misconception-based check on the idea formed from yesterday's experience, before Practice builds on it | — |
| Right after recall and concept formation | — | **The default place for the day's reflection**: short, on the concept formed today from yesterday's experience, private and written first |
| Practice | Main lesson book work as evidence (entries, drawings, tables, write-ups), read against criteria students know; a check before letting go of a new skill | Self-assessment of main lesson book work with a single-point rubric |
| New Content | Nothing: students observe, record, draw and wonder; nothing about today's experience is assessed or discussed | Nothing. **Never a reflection directly after New Content** |
| End of day | — | Only when Practice follows New Content that day, and only on the Practice (never on the new experience) |
| Last day of the main lesson block | The class's summary of key ideas in its own words as evidence | End-of-block reflection at Recall, with Rawson's three steps |

**Rawson's three steps** (Rawson 2021, p. 91), for working-process and end-of-block reflection, with criteria students understand and aimed at the will to do better:

1. What happened, and what should have happened?
2. What differences and surprises were there?
3. What will I do better next time?

**Block day 1** has no recall; its open look back at the previous main lesson block in this subject is not assessed. **Four-day cycle** (only when the teacher chose it): the same rules apply by role; reflection follows the day's concept phase, never the new experience.

## Subject lessons

- Assessment for learning at key points of the 5E cycle: retrieval practice at the start of a short session; a check for understanding in Explain before letting go; a hinge question where the next phase depends on an idea (often between Explain and Elaborate). Never scored.
- **Exit slips are allowed at the end of any session** as assessment as learning ("What I can now explain, and what is still unclear"), private and written, never scored.
- Scored Evaluate work belongs to the cycle plan and the Thai core plan's rubrics; this skill only plans the formative side and points to it.

## Channels

Kreng jai makes quiet students look disengaged in public channels, so:

1. **Private written first.** Self-assessment, reflection and "how well do I understand" go to a private written channel: the main lesson book margin, an exit card, a folded slip, a learning log only the teacher reads.
2. **Peer assessment only after shared criteria.** Peers comment only once the criteria have been shared with the class (given on a block sheet, or agreed with the class before work is exchanged), as feedback against those criteria: one strength, one question or next step. Peers never score each other.
3. **Public signals only for low-stakes checks.** Hands, thumbs, mini-whiteboards held up, letter cards or a quick show of fingers are for checks where being wrong costs nothing (which option on a hinge question, ready to move on); never for students to show publicly how well they understood or how they judge themselves. With answer cards, everyone shows at once.

## Flags

Read the teacher's plan for these and report each one in the assessment plan's **Flags** section and in one line of the reply, with the fix. Never edit the teacher's plan.

| In the teacher's plan | Fix to propose |
|---|---|
| A reflection directly after New Content (including one at the close of a day whose last part is New Content) | Move it to right after the next day's recall and concept formation, or to right after that day's own recall and concept formation on yesterday's experience; today's experience stays un-analysed |
| A quiz, test or closed questions at Recall | Open recall questions; any check moves to concept formation or Practice as a hinge question |
| A score, points or grade on a reflection, self-assessment, peer assessment or exit slip | Keep it unscored; the Thai core plan names it as assessment as learning (การประเมินเพื่อการเรียนรู้ของตนเอง, ไม่ให้คะแนน) |
| Peer scoring, or peer assessment before criteria are shared | Share or agree the criteria first; peers give feedback, not scores |
| Self-assessment of understanding through a public signal | A private written channel; the public signal only for a low-stakes check |
| End-of-block reflection after the last activity of the last day | At Recall, with Rawson's three steps |

Do not flag an end-of-day reflection when Practice follows New Content that day and the reflection is on the Practice; say it is placed correctly.
