# Thai core plans for main-lesson days

One Thai core plan per main-lesson day of a main lesson block. This file says what each plan is written from, how the Waldorf day lands on ขั้นนำ / ขั้นสอน / ขั้นสรุป, and what to check against the Waldorf plans. The section-by-section form rules are in `filling-the-form.md`.

## Sources

Read these at the layout's mapped locations before writing:

1. **Block plan** (`block and week plans`): units in order, the Thai indicators the block claims and any sequencing differences, grade mode, target ideas, the previous block in this subject. Without a block plan, write nothing: say the block needs planning first with `planning-main-lesson-blocks`.
2. **Week plan** for each week in scope: the day's row (objectives, parts in order, UDL options, preparation). Without it, say so and send the teacher to `planning-main-lesson-blocks` for that week; write nothing for its days. In an **anticipated route**, write only for fixed units and units the driving question board has already chosen; never for a unit still open.
3. **Day plan** for each day in scope (`session plans`), if it exists: the main source. Its parts, order, times, recall questions, objectives, UDL options and assessment go into the Thai core plan.
4. **No day plan for a day:** write the Thai core plan from the week plan's row, in the two-day rhythm the row shows, with times split from the class profile's session length (about 10 minutes Rhythmic Time, 40–50 minutes recall and concept formation, the rest across reflection, practice, new experience and close). Say in the reply that these plans follow the week plan because the day plans are not written yet, and offer the day plans through `planning-main-lesson-blocks`; a Thai core plan written this way should be checked again once the day plan exists.

## Naming and numbering

- **Plan number:** แผนการจัดการเรียนรู้ที่ = the block day number (block day 1 is the first session of week 1; Monday of week 2 in a five-day week is block day 6), unless the course's existing Thai core plans already number differently, in which case continue theirs.
- **Unit:** หน่วยการเรียนรู้ที่ and its เรื่อง = the block plan's unit for that day (number and title in Thai). **Plan title:** the day's focus in Thai, from the week-plan objective (e.g. "การเคลื่อนที่ของอากาศเมื่อได้รับความร้อน").
- **Time:** the session length from the day plan or class profile, written exactly (100 minutes: `1 ชั่วโมง 40 นาที`), never rounded.
- **Files** in a folder location: `day-01-thai-core-plan.md`, `day-02-thai-core-plan.md`, … (two-digit block day).

## Stage mapping

Section 7's mapping in `thai-core-plan-template.th.md` applies, read through the two-day rhythm (ADR-0003: the mapping is unchanged; the order inside ขั้นสอน is the day's own). For a steady-state day:

| Section 7 stage | What it lists, in the day plan's order |
|---|---|
| ขั้นนำเข้าสู่บทเรียน | Rhythmic Time (กิจกรรมวงกลม); Maths/Literacy if used; the **recall** of yesterday's experience: its open questions as asked, written then pairs then class. Never a quiz, test or closed check |
| ขั้นสอน/ขั้นจัดกิจกรรม | **Concept formation** on yesterday's experience: each student takes a position (claim, sketch model), the class compares and integrates them; questions added to the driving question board; the target idea worked toward, never the concept to be told. Then the **reflection** (assessment as learning, not scored), **Practice** (one shared task with its UDL options), and **today's new experience**: what students observe, record and draw, explicitly left un-analysed until tomorrow |
| ขั้นสรุป | The **close**: students note what they observed and wonder about; the teacher says what tomorrow's recall starts from. Add one Thai line for the supervisor that the day's knowledge was summarised during concept formation in ขั้นสอน and that today's new experience is analysed in tomorrow's lesson (e.g. "การสรุปองค์ความรู้ของวันนี้เกิดขึ้นในช่วงการสร้างความคิดรวบยอดในขั้นสอน ส่วนประสบการณ์ใหม่ของวันนี้จะนำมาทบทวนและอภิปรายในแผนการจัดการเรียนรู้ถัดไป") |

Number the steps within each stage (1, 2, 3 …) with their minutes, as IPST's numbered learning steps do, and put each step's UDL option beside it in brackets. The ชิ้นงาน/ภาระงาน list is the day's main lesson book entries and other products.

**ขั้นสรุป never analyses that day's new experience**, whatever the day plan says: no explanation, no "why" question, no worksheet, no discussion, no exit card on it. If the day plan does any of this, see the [consistency check](#consistency-check).

## Day 1, the last day and the four-day cycle

- **Block day 1:** ขั้นนำ holds Rhythmic Time and the **link to the previous block** in this subject (named from the course; otherwise "บล็อกก่อนหน้าในวิชานี้" with a note for the teacher to name it), not a recall. ขั้นสอน holds the short preview of the journey, Practice if any (main lesson book set-up), and the **anchoring phenomenon** as the new experience, left un-analysed. ขั้นสรุป closes: tomorrow starts from what students experienced. K objectives on day 1 are about noticing and describing (observation, questions), since no concept is formed yet.
- **Day 2:** ขั้นสอน starts with students writing their own questions and the class building the driving question board, then concept formation as far as it goes.
- **Last day of the block:** no new experience. ขั้นสอน holds concept formation on yesterday's experience and putting the pieces together at the anchoring phenomenon and the driving question; ขั้นสรุป may hold the block reflection and students' own summary, since no new experience needs protecting.
- **Four-day cycle** (only when the day plans use it): ขั้นนำ holds Rhythmic Time and the recall of E(n−1), open questions only; ขั้นสอน holds concept formation on E(n−2), application of C(n−3), and the new experience E(n), each labelled as in the day plan; ขั้นสรุป as above.

## Consistency check

Compare every Thai core plan with its sources. Report each finding in the reply (which day, which part, what differs) and say which way the Thai core plan went. Never change a Waldorf plan unasked.

| Check | If it fails |
|---|---|
| The Thai core plan has the same parts, in the same order, with the same experiences as the day plan (or week-plan row) | Fix the Thai core plan to follow the Waldorf plan |
| The day plan agrees with its week-plan row (experiences, order, objectives) | Report the disagreement; the Thai core plan follows the day plan, the more detailed source |
| The day plan's times add up to the class profile's session length | Report the total against the session length; write the Thai core plan's time as the session length and keep the day plan's split, noting the excess for the teacher |
| The indicators in the Thai core plan are among those the block plan claims (or its standards map) | Use only claimed indicators; if the day's work needs another, report it and offer to add it to the block plan |
| The day plan breaks the two-day rhythm in any of three ways: (a) it discusses, analyses, explains or tests that day's new experience (a "why" question, a worksheet, a marked task, a discussion straight after it, a close that explains it); (b) Recall is a quiz or a closed check; (c) a reflection follows New Content directly | Handle all three the same way. Report each one (which day, which part) and say plainly why it breaks the rhythm: a new experience is left to settle overnight, and tomorrow's open recall picks it up. Recommend fixing the day plan first and offer to do it (open recall questions; the analysis or reflection moved to the next day, after recall); never change it unasked. If the day plan's Notes record the departure as the teacher's choice, report it without recommending a change. Until the day plan changes, the Thai core plan follows it: describe each part truthfully where the day plan has it, in ขั้นนำ or ขั้นสอน but never in ขั้นสรุป (anything the day plan does with the new experience at its close goes at the end of ขั้นสอน), and never score it |
| Assessment in the day plan scores assessment as learning (reflections, self-assessment) | Report it; the Thai core plan names it without a score |

Why the rhythm breaks are handled alike: the Thai core plan and the Waldorf plan must agree, so the fix belongs in the day plan, not in a Thai core plan that quietly differs from it.
