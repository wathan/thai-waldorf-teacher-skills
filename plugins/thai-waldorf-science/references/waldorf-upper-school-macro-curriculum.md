# Waldorf Upper-School Macro-Curriculum (Grades 9–12)

> **Status:** A **working synthesis offered as an ideal type** (Bransby & Rawson). It is orientation, expected to change through use, and will give way if a more authoritative upper-school source is published. It is *not* a transcription of any single document: no grade-by-grade, content-free upper-school macro-curriculum is openly published.
>

## 1. Overall journey

Adolescence as a whole = **“the question for truth.”** The four years are four angles on it:

| Grade | Question | Trained capacity | Relation reorganised | Motif (meso; localise) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **9** | **What?** | Observation | Body ↔ World (the given) | Unresolved opposition |
| **10** | **How?** | Comparison | Self ↔ System (the related) | Polarity and intensification |
| **11** | **Why?** | Analysis | Self-as-knower ↔ World's constituents (the individual, the invisible) | The invisible / the individual element |
| **12** | **Who?** | Synthesis | Self ↔ Whole world, consciously held (the integrated) | Comprehensive synthesis |

The structure is a spiral. Grade 12 consciously recapitulates grade 1's unreflective sense of a whole world: “the image of the whole,” now known “for the first time.”

## 2. Grade modes (lookup cards)

### Grade 9: *What?* (ม.3)
- **Question:** What is here? What happened? What is actually going on?
- **Capacity:** Observation. Exact, theory-free description of phenomena as given, *before* explanation. Trust in one's own perception is *cultivated*, not assumed.
- **Development:** Puberty brings intense bodily experience as abstract thought awakens, experienced as turbulence or revolt. **Health and wellbeing** is acutely at stake. Re-establishing trust in one's body and in the directly met world comes before everything else.
- **Relation:** Body ↔ World.
- **Epistemic stance:** Perception is taken at face value. Describe and record before theorising. **Enquiry** is descriptive (“What is actually here?”). **Judgement** is about accuracy (“Is this observation complete and honest?”).
- **Motif:** Unresolved opposition, e.g. heat/cold, expansion/contraction, revolution/old order, colliding plates. Experience and describe it; resolution belongs to grade 10.
- **Spiral:** Continues grade 8's technological and mechanical grasp of the world, now coloured by puberty.
- **Design cues:**
  - Lead with phenomena and exact records.
  - Make students separate observation from inference.
  - Stage contrasts but do not resolve them.
  - Require careful drawing and description.
- **Avoid:** Asking for hypotheses or models of the unobservable as a requirement.

### Grade 10: *How?* (ม.4)
- **Question:** How does this relate to that? How do opposing things interact, and what do they produce together?
- **Capacity:** Comparison. Weigh contrasting phenomena for their relative value, origin and significance. **Holistic thinking** emerges as grasping how opposites belong to one dynamic system. **Aesthetics** (balance, proportion, fit) is both felt and a cognitive tool.
- **Development:** Turbulence settles into equilibrium as students search for a personal “fulcrum” between extremes. Wellbeing stabilises. **Future thinking** begins to reach beyond the self into systems and patterns.
- **Relation:** Self ↔ System.
- **Epistemic stance:** Relational, polar reasoning: two terms as poles of one system whose interaction generates something new (intensification). **Enquiry:** “How do these connect?” **Judgement:** “Which interpretation best fits the relationship?” **Empathy** works epistemically: enter each pole before judging between them.
- **Motif:** Polarity and intensification, e.g. acid/base, centripetal/centrifugal, high/low pressure. Grade 9's oppositions resolve, not by eliminating one pole but by recognising generative tension.
- **Spiral:** Establishes the systems and wholes whose constituents grade 11 will analyse.
- **Design cues:**
  - Fair comparison with controlled variables.
  - Paired phenomena.
  - System diagrams.
  - Present opposing positions with genuine empathy before comparing them.
- **Avoid:** Requiring an owned, defended stance. Treating polarity as mandatory when the phenomenon lacks it.

### Grade 11: *Why?* (ม.5)
- **Question:** Why are things this way? At its deepest reach this means destiny, meaning and responsibility, where it shades into “Who?” (§4).
- **Capacity:** Analysis. Independent, abstract theorising: resolving a whole into its constituents. **Imagination** becomes theoretical: coherent mental models of the unperceivable (atom, field, cell). This is disciplined construction, possible *only* because of the observational (g9) and relational (g10) groundwork.
- **Development:** New inner depth and individuality bring an urge to break from what was given and find one's own course. **Future thinking:** “This is my own course, I must find and take it.” **Democratic participation** becomes critical: whose voice is heard, and why? Wellbeing is a stable background that makes inward, theoretical work possible.
- **Relation:** Self-as-knower ↔ World's constituents. Two movements: outward to the smallest units (element, atom, cell, individual historical actor) and inward to the self as model-builder.
- **Epistemic stance:** **Meta-conceptual competence.** Models of the unobservable become accessible and trustworthy, and are recognised as **constructions, not transcriptions**. **Enquiry** is hypothetical and analytical. **Judgement:** “Which model best explains the evidence, and what are its limits?” **Senses** become inferential: attending to the *effects* of the unperceivable.
- **Motif:** The invisible and the individual element, e.g. atomic structure, electricity, cell theory, the periodic table, the solitary quester (Grail, Renaissance), the point at infinity in projective geometry.
- **Spiral:** Returns to grade 7's practical and concrete themes, now abstract and theoretical.
- **Design cues:**
  - Build a model, test it against observable effects, compare it with a rival model, state its limits.
  - Ask whose interests and voices shape the knowledge.
- **Avoid:** Purely descriptive work (under-challenge). Models without an observational base (missing preconditions). “Who”-material required rather than offered as an optional stretch.

### Grade 12: *Who?* (ม.6)
- **Question:** Who is this being called human? Who or what stands behind the whole? Pursued far enough it becomes “Why?” (§4).
- **Capacity:** Synthesis. Draw previously analysed, separate elements and disciplines into a coherent whole. **Holistic thinking** becomes explicit and conscious. **Imagination** is at its widest: the planet, the species, the epoch or one's whole biography as a single image.
- **Development:** Near full maturity. A panoramic, retrospective view of one's own education. **Future thinking** is long-range and ethical: “What is my responsibility toward the world I am entering?” **Democratic participation** becomes active: take a considered position and contribute. **Lifelong learning** becomes conscious: education is a start, not a completion.
- **Relation:** Self ↔ Whole world, consciously held. Wholeness returns (compare grade 1) but is achieved through synthesis, with the self within and responsible to the whole. Wellbeing is integrated with cognitive and social life.
- **Epistemic stance:** Synthetic, holistic judgement. Integrate models (including those built in grade 11) into a coherent picture, owned as the student's own achievement. **Enquiry:** “Who or what stands behind this whole?” **Judgement:** synthetic and ethical (“What does this situation call for? What is my responsibility?”). **Senses:** integrative.
- **Motif:** Comprehensive synthesis, e.g. world history, a survey of architecture, the animal kingdom culminating in the human being, biochemistry reuniting grade 11's elements.
- **Spiral:** Recapitulates grade 1, “the image of the whole,” known consciously.
- **Design cues:**
  - Cross-disciplinary integrating tasks.
  - Hold several models in relation.
  - Students' own positions defended within a community.
  - Reflection on their own trajectory as knowers.
- **Avoid:** Forcing synthetic mode on every task. Earlier modes stay valid where they carry the work.

## 3. Potentiality-mode matrix (vertical development)

Potentialities are exercised in **every** grade. What changes is the *predominant mode*. Use this to check whether a task demands the right mode for its grade.

| Potentiality | G9 | G10 | G11 | G12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Senses** | Sharpened attention; direct perception is where knowing starts | Comparative: how phenomena differ and relate | Inferential: the *effects* of the unperceivable | Integrative: the whole through its sensory parts |
| **Imagination** | Exact and concrete: vivid description of the observed | Relational: the dynamic interaction of poles | Theoretical: coherent models of invisible entities | Holistic: planet, species, epoch as one image |
| **Enquiry** | Descriptive: “What is actually here?” | Relational: “How do these connect?” | Analytical/hypothetical: “Why is it this way?” | Synthetic: “Who or what stands behind this whole?” |
| **Holistic thinking** | Implicit: wholeness experienced, not grasped | Emerging: opposites in one dynamic system | Analytical: whole temporarily dissolved into constituents | Explicit: parts consciously re-integrated into a responsible whole |
| **Judgement** | Descriptive: “Is this observation accurate and complete?” | Comparative: “Which interpretation best fits the relationship?” | Analytical/evaluative: “Which model best explains the evidence, and what are its limits?” | Synthetic/ethical: “What does this situation call for? What is my responsibility?” |
| **Health and wellbeing** | Central and acute: the newly matured body *is* the challenge | Stabilising: a personal fulcrum between bodily and cognitive life | Background: a stable platform for inward work | Integrative: physical, cognitive and social as one biography |
| **Future thinking** | Near-term: “What is happening to me now?” | Medium-term: self within systems and social patterns | Biographical individuation: “my own course” | Long-range: “my responsibility to the world I'm entering” |
| **Democratic participation** | Implicit: living with pluralist tension | Relational: opposing positions within a productive whole | Critical: whose voice is heard, whose model privileged, why | Active: a considered position that contributes to direction |

**Ethical ladder** (taken from the Judgement and Democratic participation rows; used by the PISA-adapted competencies):

| G9 | G10 | G11 | G12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Faithful witness:** describe each party accurately, no comparison | **Epistemic empathy:** enter each perspective, then compare | **Critical:** whose voice or interest is weighed or left out | **Owned:** take and defend a considered position in a community |

“No false balance” holds on every rung. Respect is owed to people and their concerns, not equal standing for factual claims that fail scrutiny.

- *Language and communication, Empathy, Aesthetics and Lifelong learning* are exercised throughout. They vary less by grade, so set their mode at micro level by activity and subject.
- *Modes overlap; they do not switch on and off.* A grade 9 student will do some analytical enquiry, and a grade 12 student still relies on exact observation. The matrix shows the **predominant challenge**, not exclusive capacities.

## 4. Boundary rules

- **Grade 11/12 Why/Who boundary is soft.**
  - Gerwin's grade 11 “Why” reaches into “destiny, life's meaning, social responsibility”. His grade 12 “Who” asks what “stands behind the outer play of events … pulling them together into a synthesizing whole”. At their limit these are the same question asked two ways. Some schools (e.g. San Francisco) put “Who” in grade 11.
  - Content about agency, responsibility or “who is behind this” can legitimately be placed in either year. It is not misclassified.
  - *Stricter local rule in the competency files:* grade 11 may offer Who-flavoured material only as an optional stretch, never as a requirement.
- **Spiral pairings are illustrative, not a system.** Gerwin gives 7↔11 and 8↔9 only “for example”. 12↔1 is the one he develops. Build nothing on an assumed pairing for grade 10 beyond its role preparing for grade 11.

## 5. Placement diagnostic (for any content, unit, block or lesson)

Bransby & Rawson: *“If this content is the pedagogical answer to this grade's developmental question, what is that question, and how good an answer is it?”*

1. **Fit:** Which grade's *relation reorganised* and *epistemic stance* does the content most plausibly serve?
2. **Mode check:** Does it exercise the potentialities in that grade's mode? E.g. descriptive enquiry → grade 9; hypothetical enquiry → grade 11.
3. **Misplacement:** If its natural fit is another grade, report that. It is a finding, not necessarily a fault, since some content does one grade's macro work while being timetabled in another.
4. **Under-challenge:** Potentialities exercised in an *earlier* mode than the grade warrants (e.g. purely descriptive enquiry in grade 11).
5. **Missing preconditions:** Content that *skips* earlier modes (e.g. hypothetical enquiry without the observational foundation of grades 9–10).

**Output template:**

```
Content: <...>
Best-fit grade: G_  (relation: <...>; stance: <...>)
Planned grade: G_   → fit | misplaced (note) 
Potentiality modes exercised: <potentiality: mode> ×2–4
Flags: under-challenge? missing precondition? boundary (11/12)?
Answer quality: <...>
```

## 7. Glossary

- **Cross-curricular motif:** a meso-level theme carrying a grade's developmental theme across subjects. Expected to vary by culture.
- **Developmental task:** what learners must engage with at a stage. It comes from the developing person *and* from society's requirements (Rawson 2020; Bransby & Rawson 2021).
- **Epistemic stance:** a grade's characteristic relation to knowledge and models (e.g. trusting perception in grade 9, trusting models of the unobservable in grade 11). Macro-level. The motif is its meso-level vehicle.
- **Generative principle:** a content-free pedagogical claim (Rawson 2021). Meta-level. See `rawson2021-generative-principles-upper-school.md`.
- **Horizontal / vertical / spiral structuring:** themes linked across subjects within a year / a capacity's progression across years / themes revisited at greater depth.
- **Ideal type:** a best-fit orientation, modified in use (Weber, via Rawson). This file is one.
- **Macro / meso / micro / meta:** developmental themes with no content / locally chosen content / a teacher's lesson plans / generative principles and aims.
- **Meta-conceptual competence:** holding a model as a construction rather than a transcription of reality. Emerges around grade 11.
- **Phenomenological method:** experiment and observation first, theory afterwards, grounded in them. Especially strong through grade 10.
- **Potentiality mode:** the grade-specific form in which a potentiality is exercised (§3).
- **Relational shift:** each grade's change in how the young person relates to body, self, others and world. The defining content of the macro level.
- **The question for truth:** the single adolescent disposition behind all four grades.
