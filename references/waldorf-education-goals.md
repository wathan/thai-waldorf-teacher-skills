# Waldorf Education Goals (Meta-Level Aims)

> **Sources:** Bransby, K. & Rawson, M. (2021), *Waldorf Education for the Future: A framework for curriculum practice*, SWSF, for the 12 potentialities, the potentiality → capacity cycle and the three-domain aims. Biesta, G. (2013), *The Beautiful Risk of Education*, for the three domains. Steiner (1919/2020), *The First Teachers' Course*, for the founding aim.
>
> **What this is:** Waldorf's aims stated on their own terms, *before* any alignment with outside frameworks (PISA, Thai standards). Alignment comes later, as a separate analytical step. Never edit the goals to fit an external framework.
>
> **Agent use:**
> - Use as the **top of the justification chain**: every block or lesson must trace to ≥1 domain and ≥1 potentiality here.
> - Use the potentiality list as the **vocabulary for "what is being developed"**. Prefer it over "competence".
> - Use the diagnostic in §5 to justify or reject content.
>
> **Related files:** `waldorf-upper-school-macro-curriculum.md` (grade modes of each potentiality, grades 9–12) · `rawson2021-generative-principles-upper-school.md` (how to teach) · `waldorf-pisa-competencies-g9-g12.md` (external competencies adapted by grade).

## 1. The founding aim

> “The question should not be: What does a human being need to know and be able to do for the social order that now exists? but rather: What capacities are latent in this human being, and what lies within that can be developed? Then it will be possible to bring ever new forces into the social order from the rising generations.” (Steiner 1919, cited in Bransby & Rawson 2021)

- **Direction:** education starts from the developing person and works outward to the social order, not from society's requirements inward to the person.
- **Intended graduate:** someone able to *renew* the social order, not someone fitted to it.
- **Curriculum, methods and assessment are all instruments of this aim.**

## 2. Three domains of purpose (Biesta, adopted by Bransby & Rawson)

All three are always held together and none reduces to another. An education that achieves only one or two of them is incomplete.

| Domain | Definition | Waldorf emphasis | Design implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Qualification** | Dispositions, skills, knowledge and cultural capital to participate in and contribute to work and civil society | “Knowledgeable action with purpose” across practical, social, artistic and academic fields. A basis for participating, not an end in itself. A skill without its purpose, or knowledge without a responsible disposition to use it, does not count. | Tasks are authentic applications with a clear *why*. No skill drills cut off from purpose. |
| **Socialisation** | Dispositions and abilities to participate in a community and a multicultural society; coherent identities | Both reproducing *and renewing* social practices. Intercultural sensitivity, democratic participation, working with diverse others. Identity is an ongoing biographical achievement. The class as a community of practice is the main site. | The quality of classroom relationships is curriculum. Design collaboration, roles and intercultural perspectives. |
| **Individuation (subjectification)** | Agency, judgement, sense of coherence, responsibility, ethics, life orientation, creativity, a relationship to spirituality | The most distinctively Waldorf domain and the closest to the founding aim. Authority passes step by step from teacher, curriculum and tradition to the student's own judgement. **Upper school is the phase where individuation becomes the explicit task.** | Every block includes moments where students form, justify and own a judgement, scaled to the grade (see the macro file's ethical ladder). |

**Developmental dispositions (Bransby & Rawson):**

| Phase | Core disposition | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Early childhood (0–7) | The world is good | Will: healthy habits, trust |
| Primary years (7–14) | The world is beautiful | Feeling: aesthetic and relational engagement |
| **Adolescence (14–21)** | **The question for truth** | **Thinking: autonomous judgement and enquiry** |

“The question for truth” does not mean adolescents possess truth. It means they are disposed to seek it: to form questions, weigh evidence, take positions and stay open to revising them.

## 3. The twelve potentialities: what is developed

- **Definition:** “the unique ways human beings relate to the world and which form the basis for people to act in meaningful and meaning-making ways” (Bransby & Rawson 2021).
- **Ideal types, not a competency checklist.** They are lenses for orientation and for monitoring actual development. They are innate as potential and emerge through education, with no upper limit.
- **Growth path:** potentialities → skills → dispositions → enhanced capacities → new potentialities, repeated across the whole school career at increasing depth and autonomy.
- **Domain affinities** show where each potentiality is most central. Most serve all three domains.

| # | Potentiality | Primary domain(s) | Core meaning | Upper-school design cue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Language and communication** | Soc, Qual | Orality, literacy, symbolism, digital and media literacy, a language beyond the mother tongue. Language gives *voice*. | Presenting, debating, written registers (factual, analytical, creative), work in a second language |
| 2 | **Health, wellbeing, bodily integrity** | Ind | Feeling at home in one's body. Basic needs met. **Sense of coherence** (comprehensible, manageable, meaningful). Trusting relationships. The precondition for everything else. | Avoid overload. Check coherence indicators. Hold a healthy rhythm. |
| 3 | **Senses** | Qual | Refined, integrated senses. Directing attention to what is salient. Resonance (Rosa). Grounds the phenomenological method: observation is a cultivated capacity. | Exact observation tasks, especially in grade 9. Sensory attention is an explicit aim. |
| 4 | **Imagination** | Qual, Ind | Picturing what is not present (history, hypotheses, futures, solutions). Creative, playful engagement. Developed *before* abstraction. | Vivid narrative. Mental models of the unobservable (grade 11). Holding the whole (grade 12). |
| 5 | **Empathy** | Soc | Embodied perception of others' states and intentions. Basis for collaboration across difference and on “wicked problems”. Also a way of knowing: participatory knowledge. | Enter each perspective before comparing (grade 10's epistemic empathy) |
| 6 | **Aesthetics** | Soc, Ind | Experiencing the inherent qualities of things and responding with meaning. The aesthetics of other cultures as intercultural understanding. Art as a way of knowing. | Careful main lesson books. Designing and making. Studying art from many cultures. |
| 7 | **Enquiry** | Ind, Qual | Questions from genuine curiosity that “open situations up and generate knowledge”. Basis of science, hermeneutics, symptomatology and research. Includes confidence that one's own questions are worth pursuing. | Students generate questions from phenomena. Mode by grade: descriptive → relational → hypothetical → synthetic. |
| 8 | **Democratic participation** | Soc | Participation in communities of practice. Recognising what hinders, manipulates or perverts democracy. Self-management. | Group decisions, roles, gradual handover of responsibility, critique of whose voice counts |
| 9 | **Lifelong learning** | Ind | Learning as transformation, not retention. **Expansive** learning that widens one's agency. Metacognition. | Self-assessment, ipsative feedback, reflection on one's own learning process |
| 10 | **Future thinking** | Ind, Soc | Navigating life pathways, biographical opportunities, self-chosen aspirations. Sensing the emerging future. Wicked problems. | Upper school: intentional biographical orientation. Time horizons widen by grade. |
| 11 | **Holistic thinking and spiritual experience** | Ind, Qual | Systemic and ecological thinking. Moving between scales without losing either end. The experience of being part of a larger whole that gives ethical orientation. | Systems thinking. Integrative synthesis (grade 12). Moments of wonder and meaning. |
| 12 | **Judgement** | Ind | Autonomous conclusions (logical, aesthetic, ethical) “based on insight and on the weighing up of numerous factors”. Practical wisdom for the common good. Taking, justifying and explaining a position. The culmination of individuation: Steiner's “free human being”. | Every block: weigh evidence → position → justify. Scaled by grade. |

## 4. The intended graduate (end of grade 12, ~age 18)

- Potentialities have grown into **enhanced capacities**: stable, general and available across contexts without scaffolding.
- The three domains are held together: participates in work and civil society, belongs to and contributes to communities, acts from autonomous judgement.
- “The question for truth” is a **stable orientation**: seeking understanding, weighing evidence, forming considered views, staying open to revision.
- Potentialities are **usable**, never “mastered”. The graduate can spot and pursue their own further development.
- **The portrait is non-metric by design.** Bransby & Rawson prefer “potentialities” and “enhanced capacities” to OECD/DeSeCo “competence” because that term's meanings shift. Describe qualitatively, assess narratively, understand biographically.

## 5. Where the goals sit in the curriculum

| Level | Content | Source in repo |
|---|---|---|
| **Meta** | Generative principles and overall aims (this file) | `waldorf-education-goals.md`, `rawson2021-generative-principles-upper-school.md` |
| **Macro** | Developmental themes by grade, with no fixed content | `waldorf-upper-school-macro-curriculum.md` |
| **Meso** | National or school content chosen as “possible answers” to macro questions | Thai: `thai-science-core-indicators-2560.md`, `thai-science-supplementary-learning-outcomes-2560.md`. PISA-derived: `waldorf-pisa-competencies-g9-g12.md` |
| **Micro** | A teacher's lesson plans for one class | Generated output |

**Rule: content is always instrumental.** Justify any content by (a) the potentialities it develops, (b) the macro-level developmental task it serves and (c) the domain(s) it serves, never by tradition alone. Bransby & Rawson's diagnostic:

> **“What developmental question is this content an answer to, and how good an answer is it?”**

**Justification template for agents:**

```
Content: <topic / activity>
Domain(s): Qualification | Socialisation | Individuation
Potentialities (1–3): <#, name — mode at this grade>
Macro task (grade N): <guiding question / relational shift it serves>
Meso anchor: <Thai indicator code | PISA Sx.n> (optional, local/statutory layer)
Answer quality: <why this content is a good answer; what a better one might be>
```

## 6. Glossary

- **Domain (Biesta):** one of qualification, socialisation, individuation. All three are held simultaneously.
- **Enhanced capacity:** a potentiality after repeated exercise. Stable, general and transferable.
- **Ideal type:** an analytic lens for orientation and monitoring, not a fixed endpoint or a test profile.
- **Individuation:** becoming a self-determining subject who acts from autonomous judgement (Biesta's “subjectification”).
- **Knowledgeable action with purpose:** Bransby & Rawson's term for “skill”: knowledge applied meaningfully in authentic situations, understanding why and to what end.
- **Potentiality:** a unique human way of relating to the world. Innate as potential, emergent through education, with no upper limit.
- **The question for truth:** the core adolescent disposition. Seeking truth, not possessing it.
- **Sense of coherence (salutogenesis):** Antonovsky's measure of whether learning and life are comprehensible, manageable and meaningful. A curriculum responsibility.
- **Telos:** the ultimate aim. In Waldorf, the “free human being” who acts from considered insight rather than external authority.
